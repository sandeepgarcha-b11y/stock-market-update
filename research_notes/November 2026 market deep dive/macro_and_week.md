## 2. Bank of England decision, Thu 5 Nov 2026

### Takeaway
The BoE **raised Bank Rate 25bp to 4.00%** (from 3.75%) on an **8–1 vote**, with the lone dissenter (reported as Catherine Mann) wanting **+50bp to 4.25%**. It is the first hike since August 2023. The Bank blamed Middle East–driven energy prices and sees CPI peaking near **5% by mid-2027**. Reuters reported that at least one more increase is likely if the US–Iran conflict persists. December hike odds rose to ~50%. The gilt and sterling reaction was modest, because the hike was fully priced.

### Cited Findings
- The MPC voted 8–1 to raise Bank Rate by 0.25pp to 4%; one member voted for +0.5pp to 4.25%. — [Bank of England, Nov 2026 Monetary Policy Summary](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy-summary-and-minutes/2026/november-2026); [MoneyWeek live](https://moneyweek.com/economy/news/live/uk-interest-rates-november-bank-of-england)
- Trading Economics identifies the 50bp dissenter as Catherine Mann; it is the first increase since August 2023. — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-kingdom/interest-rate)
- **Rationale (BoE):** "energy prices have stayed high and volatile due to conflict in the Middle East"; inflation is expected to reach **about 5% by mid-2027**. The minutes say the energy price increase "generated sufficient inflationary pressures in the pipeline to warrant an increase in Bank Rate". — [BoE](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy-summary-and-minutes/2026/november-2026)

### Inferences
- The 8–1 split, with the dissent on the hawkish side, is more hawkish than the September 6–3 hold. The MPC has moved from "whether" to "how fast".
- The ~5% mid-2027 CPI peak compares with 3.5% in September. Unless energy prices fall, UK real rates stay low and further hikes stay live.
- This week's ~12% fall in oil (section 6) could cut against that hawkishness if it lasts. The forecasts were likely conditioned on pre-midterm energy futures (inference only; the conditioning date was not confirmed).
- For UK savers and mortgage holders, 4% Bank Rate and a ~50% chance of 4.25% in December mean cash rates stay attractive and fixed mortgage rates stay elevated.
