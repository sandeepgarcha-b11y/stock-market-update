## Q2. What drove the sell-off, and what Q3 2026 megacap AI earnings showed

### Takeaway
The October sell-off came from several pressures acting together:
- **AI capex and free-cash-flow doubts:** Alphabet and Amazon both reported heavy negative free cash flow, and Alphabet missed estimates.
- **AI credit stress:** CoreWeave debt worries.
- **A semiconductor rout:** Intel fell ~29% on 30 Oct.
- **Oil above $110 and a bond rout:** the US 10y reached its highest level since April 2002, around 5%.
- **Hawkish central banks:** two Fed dissents in favour of a hike.

Microsoft was the relative bright spot (Azure +48%), but it also *trimmed* its capex forecast, which CNBC framed as the AI buildout hitting a "snag".

## Q3. Current valuation levels

### Takeaway
On a *forward* basis US equities have **de-rated sharply**: the S&P 500 is at **18.7x** forward earnings (FactSet, 30 Oct 2026), *below* both its 5-year (19.8) and 10-year (19.1) averages and well below 23.1x a year earlier, because earnings have grown faster than prices. On a *trailing/cyclical* basis the market is still very expensive: the Shiller CAPE is about **40–42**, near the 1999 peak of about 44.2.

### Cited Findings
- **FactSet S&P 500 forward 12-month P/E**: 18.7 (30 Oct 2026), 18.7 (23 Oct), 19.0 (16 Oct and 30 Sep, end of Q3) and 19.1 (9 Oct). The 5-year average is **19.8** and the 10-year average is **19.1**. A year earlier (29 Oct 2025) FactSet reported **23.1**. — [FactSet Earnings Insight](https://www.factset.com/earningsinsight); [FactSet, 23 Oct 2026](https://insight.factset.com/sp-500-earnings-season-update-october-23-2026); [FactSet, 16 Oct 2026](https://insight.factset.com/sp-500-earnings-season-update-october-16-2026); [FactSet Earnings Insight PDF, 9 Oct 2026](https://advantage.factset.com/hubfs/Website/Resources%20Section/Research%20Desk/Earnings%20Insight/EarningsInsight_100926.pdf)

### Inferences
- The gap between a forward P/E below average and a CAPE near record means that **valuation depends on AI-era earnings holding up**. CAPE uses 10 years of inflation-adjusted earnings, so it is inflated when earnings have grown fast recently. If AI-driven earnings disappoint, the "cheap" forward multiple is illusory.
- GuruFocus's "long-term average 32.68" appears to use a short recent window. The commonly cited Shiller mean since 1881 is in the high teens (background knowledge, not verified this session). On either basis the current CAPE is historically very high.
- Implied forward EPS of about $405 (7,580 / 18.7, my arithmetic) is consistent with StreetStats' $410.97.
- With the US 10y at about 5%, the S&P forward earnings yield (1/18.7 ≈ 5.3%) is barely above the risk-free yield. The equity risk premium is thin by historical standards (my inference).

## Q4. Interest rates, bond yields and UK inflation

### Takeaway
This is a **rising-rate environment** driven by an energy shock:
- **Fed:** held at **3.75–4.00%** on 28 Oct 2026 after a September hike, with two dissents in favour of another hike. A December hike is partly priced.
- **BoE:** at **3.75%**, with a hike to **4.00% about 94% priced** for **Thu 5 Nov 2026**.
- **UK CPI:** rose to **3.5%** in September 2026, and the BoE sees it slightly above 4% in Q1 2027.
- **US 10y Treasury:** about **5.0%**.
- **UK gilts:** the 10y is about 4.9–5.3% (sources conflict). The 30y briefly topped **6%** in early October, for the first time since 1998.

### Cited Findings
