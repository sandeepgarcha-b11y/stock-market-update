# Chip Crash Goes Global, Britain Holds Firm

Last week's narrow US tech dip became a broader, sharper global sell-off on Thursday 5 November. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5% and entered a **correction** (a fall of 10% or more from its high). The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) dropped **12.3%, its worst day since March 2020**. On Friday 6 November Japan's Nikkei closed in a **bear market** (20% or more below its peak), and South Korea had to halt trading after the KOSPI fell more than 8%. The trigger was fear that the Democrats' sweep of Congress on 3 November will bring tighter rules on AI data centres, on top of October's doubts about whether AI spending will pay off. The heaviest falls came in the year's biggest winners, the memory and storage makers: Sandisk fell 22% and Micron 17.5% on the day, just after both reported the most profitable quarters in their history. Britain and Europe held up. The FTSE 100 and STOXX 600 traded near record highs on Thursday morning and closed less than 0.5% lower. Their heavy weighting in energy, banks, pharma and miners helped, even as the Bank of England raised rates to 4% and oil fell about 12% in the week on hopes of a US–Iran deal.

For the £14,000 ISA plan in the 1 November report, the spread across different assets did its job. The rule of thumb for investing faster (a 10% fall in the global market) has probably not been hit. My estimate puts MSCI World about 6–7% below its high, but no source gives an exact figure. The sensible course is therefore to keep the staged schedule. Step 2 falls around 19–20 November, after Nvidia's results on 17 November and UK inflation figures on 18 November. Check how far VWRP is below its high on your platform, and bring the remaining steps forward only if it reaches −10%.

Memory stocks now trade at about 3–6 times forward earnings. That looks like a bargain, but it is exactly how markets price a boom-and-bust industry they believe is at peak profits. If you want memory exposure, fit it inside the existing 10% satellite rather than adding it on top.

## A rate hike in London, a sweep in Washington and cheaper oil

The **Bank of England raised Bank Rate from 3.75% to 4.00% on 5 November**, its first increase since August 2023 ([Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy-summary-and-minutes/2026/november-2026); [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-kingdom/interest-rate)). The vote was 8–1, and the one dissenter, reported as Catherine Mann, wanted a bigger rise to 4.25%. The Bank blamed energy prices that "have stayed high and volatile due to conflict in the Middle East". It now expects UK inflation to reach **about 5% by mid-2027**, against 3.5% in September.

Reuters reported that at least one more rise is likely if the US–Iran conflict continues ([AOL/Reuters](https://www.aol.com/articles/instant-view-bank-england-delivers-121730000.html)). Markets now put about a **50% chance on another rise to 4.25% on 17 December**, up from 30% before the meeting ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-05/traders-up-bets-on-faster-boe-rate-hike-pace-after-hawkish-vote)). The two-year gilt yield (the return on two-year UK government bonds) rose to about 4.76–4.79%, and the pound held near **$1.3465**, close to a seven-week high.

For a UK saver the practical effect is that cash and short-dated gilts now pay more. Money market funds, which track Bank Rate, should move towards about 4% before fees. That is my inference, so check the fund's current yield. One more point from the earlier report still applies: the 28 October Budget scrapped the planned tax on cash held inside stocks and shares ISAs ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/stocks-and-shares-isa-cash-interest-tax-cancelled-in-autumn-budget-2026-aebwl6S8X9u6)). Parking cash in the ISA while you phase in purchases therefore costs nothing in tax.

The Budget itself left the government's fiscal rules intact, with headroom cut to £15bn. Banks rallied about 3.5% because a planned rise in the bank surcharge tax was smaller than feared. Housebuilders fell about 6%, their worst day since January 2025, after a first-time-buyer scheme disappointed ([Yahoo Finance UK](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/uk-government-bonds-budget-gilts-130158581.html); [Share Talk](https://www.share-talk.com/ftse-100-edges-higher-as-uk-budget-steadies-bond-markets-but-housebuilders-slump/)). The 10-year gilt yield eased to about 5.31–5.33%. The 30-year yield had briefly topped 6% in early October, the highest since 1998 ([Admiral Markets](https://admiralmarkets.com/analytics/traders-blog/uk-gilt-yields-sell-off-gbp)).

In the US, the Federal Reserve (the US central bank) held rates at **3.75–4.00%** in late October. The vote was 10–2 and the tone hawkish, meaning it leaned towards higher rates ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2026/10/28/us-treasurys-bonds-yields-federal-reserve.html)). US inflation was 3.7% in September, mostly driven by fuel; core inflation, which excludes food and energy, was a modest 2.6% ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/september-cpi-report-inflation-energy-costs/)).

Futures markets still put about a **two-in-three chance on another Fed hike on 9 December**. That is down from about 80% a week earlier, as oil fell and shares cracked ([Pomegra](https://pomegra.io/news/fed-rate-hike-odds-slip-to-66-ahead-of-jobs-report)). The October jobs report was due on Friday afternoon UK time. Forecasts were weak, at about 59,000–65,000 new jobs and 4.2% unemployment ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/october-jobs-report-preview.html)).

The uncomfortable point is that shares are falling 4% in a day while the central bank still leans towards raising rates. In past sell-offs investors could expect the Fed to step in by cutting rates. This time it may not. A weak jobs figure combined with cheaper oil could change that quickly.

The midterms themselves were clear-cut. **Democrats won both the House and the Senate.** Seat counts were still preliminary and differed between sources ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/04/2026-midterm-election-results-live-updates.html)). The new Congress takes its seats on 3 January 2027, so President Trump keeps his war powers until then ([US News/Reuters](https://www.usnews.com/news/top-news/articles/2026-11-05/tankers-hit-in-hormuz-as-iran-keeps-up-pressure-after-us-vote)).

The market's first reaction was to price a lower chance of escalation with Iran, which meant cheaper oil, lower bond yields and stronger economically sensitive shares. Its second reaction, a day later, was to price regulatory risk for AI. The second overwhelmed the first. Gridlock between a Democratic Congress and a Republican president also makes big new tax cuts or spending packages unlikely before 2029. That is my inference.

**Oil is the master variable.** Brent crude traded as high as **$118 on 30 October**, then fell 8.7% to settle at **$94.62 on 4 November** on hopes of a deal to end the Iran war ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/energy/2026/11/04/brent-oil-slides-to-94-on-iran-uncertainty-after-us-midterms/); [FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/brent-crude-oil-price-99-90-friday-after-wall-street-ai-rout-jobs-report-iran-deal-bets-bull-103-60-bear-93-50/)). Currency moves also matter for a UK investor. The yen is weak at about 158 to the dollar. When the pound strengthens, unhedged US and Japanese holdings lose a little extra value in sterling terms.

## Memory makers earn record profits as their shares halve

**Plain-English terms used in this section:**

- **Memory chips** come in two main kinds. **DRAM** is a computer's short-term working memory. **NAND** is the flash memory that stores data in phones and solid-state drives (SSDs).
- **HBM (high-bandwidth memory)** is DRAM stacked in layers and placed right next to an AI processor. It is the scarcest and most profitable kind.
- **Hard disk drives (HDDs)**, made by Western Digital and Seagate, still hold most of the data in cloud data centres.

All of these companies have just had the best quarters in their history. That is the puzzle of this section: their shares are 30–65% below their 2026 highs anyway.

### Sandisk: a 378% revenue jump, then a 22% one-day fall

**Sandisk** best shows the gap between business results and share price. It was spun out of Western Digital in February 2025 and was the best-performing stock in the chip universe this year, up as much as roughly 575–632% at its peak ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/stocks/NASDAQ/SNDK/forecast/); [Chartrow](https://chartrow.com/lists/semiconductor-stocks)).

On 29 October it reported a quarter with about **$11.0bn of revenue**, up 378% on a year earlier. Its gross margin was 85.1%, and EPS of $49.51 beat its own guidance of $44–46 ([Motley Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/30/sandisk-sndk-q1-2027-earnings-call-transcript/)). It guided the next quarter higher, to $12.2–12.6bn of revenue and EPS of $55–57. It also announced an **8-for-1 stock split**, effective **19–20 November** ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/sandisk-q1-2027-earnings-revenue-surges-378-as-company-announces-8-for-1-stock-split)).

The shares fell about 9% the next day ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/30/sandisk-sinks-9-despite-earnings-beat-raised-guidance-and-8-for-1-split-western-digital-tumbles-8-seagate-slides-7-micron-falls-6/)). They fell again on 4 November, then **22.3% on 5 November to $806.43** ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/equities/26/11/62961405/chip-stocks-worst-day-since-2020-soxx-bear-market)). Its market value has dropped from a June record of about **$347bn to about $115bn** ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/sndk/)). The "+575% this year" figures still circulating come from before the crash; the true gain for 2026 is now much smaller.

One detail stands out. Sandisk spent $5.0bn buying back about 3.2m of its own shares in the quarter, an average of roughly $1,560 per share (calc). That is nearly double today's price.

The split changes nothing about the company's value. A holder of one share at about $806 will hold eight shares at about $101. The guided EPS of $55–57 becomes roughly $6.90–7.10 per new share. Splits sometimes attract small investors because the price per share looks lower. They do not make the business any cheaper or dearer.

Full-year revenue was **$133bn**, and adjusted EPS was $75.52, against $8.29 a year earlier ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/30/micron-mu-q4-earnings-report-2026.html); [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/11/01/why-micron-stock-looks-cheap-after-a-record-year/)). Its HBM output for 2026 is fully sold, and it plans about **$200bn** of capacity expansion ([Yahoo/Simply Wall St](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/micron-sold-2026-hbm-us-231248051.html)).

The shares fell 17.5% on 5 November, their worst session since March 2020, to about $656. They have lost nearly 30% in six trading days and are about 48% below their $1,254.61 high (calc) ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/11/05/why-micron-stock-keeps-going-down/)).

**SK Hynix**, the HBM leader, reported an operating profit of **KRW83.3trn, an 80% operating margin**, well above the KRW76.7trn forecast ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/28/sk-hynix-third-quarter-profit.html)). Net profit missed forecasts and the shares fell about 6% on the results ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-samsung-electronics-posts-record-q3-2026-profit-93CH-4981365)). The two Korean giants make up more than 40% of the KOSPI, which is why Korea's market crashed on Friday ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/27/sk-hynix-shares-ai-chip-rally-1-trillion.html)).

### Early signs that memory prices are peaking

If profits are at records, why are the shares collapsing? The answer is that the leading indicators turned before the profits did. Three signals stand out:

- **NAND prices stopped rising.** TrendForce, the industry's main price tracker, reported that NAND wafer contract prices stopped rising in October and held flat across all grades, calling it a **"high-level plateau"**. Consumer demand is weak and module makers are buying less ([TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/research/download/RP261030YB)). Only a month earlier TrendForce had forecast NAND prices rising 15–20% in the fourth quarter ([TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260930-13258.html)).
- **Kioxia guided below forecasts.** Kioxia, Sandisk's Japanese manufacturing partner, gave December-quarter profit guidance well below forecasts, citing slower price increases. Its shares fell up to 17% ([Investing.com](https://au.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/earnings-call-transcript-kioxia-posts-record-q2-2026-revenue-stock-falls-93CH-4731660)). Analysts cite this as one trigger of the rout ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/11/62943088/sandisk-leads-memory-stocks-lower-a-look-inside-the-rout)).

YMTC's third Wuhan factory ramps up in 2027. The bigger wave of supply arrives in 2027, though Chinese AI demand may absorb much of it ([TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/09/24/news-cxmt-ymtc-ramp-memory-capacity-but-chinas-ai-cloud-boom-could-soak-up-new-supply-through-2027/)). Motley Fool partly blamed Micron's slide on CXMT's plan to sell a new DRAM design as data-centre HBM ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/11/05/why-micron-stock-keeps-going-down/)). CXMT's HBM is reportedly about two generations behind.

Within the group, two differences matter:

- **Commodity NAND makers** (Sandisk, Kioxia) are where the price plateau showed up first.

Western Digital still guides to accelerating storage growth through 2028 ([WD press release](https://www.westerndigital.com/company/newsroom/press-releases/2026/2026-10-22-wd-reports-fiscal-first-quarter-2027-financial-results)). Even so, its shares are 63.8% below their June high ([TradingView/StockStory](https://www.tradingview.com/news/stockstory:74e99ca67094b:0-seagate-western-digital-vishay-intertechnology-impinj-and-allegro-microsystems-shares-plummet-what-you-need-to-know/)).

### Why a low P/E can be a trap as well as a bargain

The **forward P/E** is the share price divided by the next year's expected earnings per share. A low number usually signals a cheap share. In industries that swing between boom and bust, it often signals the opposite.

Take Micron at about $656. If its current-quarter guidance of about $38 EPS is repeated for four quarters, the P/E is about 4.3 (calc). Now suppose memory prices fall and earnings halve. The same share price becomes a P/E of about 8.6. If earnings drop 80%, as they can in a memory bust, it becomes about 21.

Share prices usually fall before earnings do. That is why boom-and-bust stocks tend to look cheapest on P/E at the top of the cycle and most expensive at the bottom. The June peaks in Sandisk and Western Digital came months before their record results, which fits that pattern. The Motley Fool headline sums up the market's view: Micron's "record $133B year leaves Wall Street pricing in a memory bust" ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/11/01/why-micron-stock-looks-cheap-after-a-record-year/)).

The opportunity is the other half of the same arithmetic. Several things support the case that this cycle will last longer than usual:

- HBM is sold under long-term contracts.
- Samsung reports its 2027 HBM output is already sold ([BigGo](https://finance.biggo.com/news/KR_005930.KS_2026-10-29)).
- SK Hynix sees shortages into 2030.
- Big tech still plans to spend more on AI in 2027.

If that holds, earnings stay high and today's multiples prove a gift. Wall Street's average Sandisk price target is still $2,056, about 2.5 times the current price ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/stocks/NASDAQ/SNDK/forecast/)). Some commentators call the sell-off a "policy-driven valuation reset" rather than a collapse in demand. Others, such as Dan Ives, warn that chip stocks could fall another 10–12% ([Invezz](https://invezz.com/en-ae/news/2026/11/05/micron-sandisk-sk-hynix-plunge-up-to-20percent-as-memory-stocks-selloff-intensifies/)).

The deciding question is not whether 4x earnings is cheap. It is what memory will cost in 2027. Watch three things:

- NAND and DRAM contract prices in November and December.
- Micron's December results.
- Big tech's 2027 spending plans, due with their January–February results.

**Networking, optical and power names** delivered strong results, and their shares still fell:

- **Astera Labs** grew revenue 154% ([Astera 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001736297/000173629726000044/q326exhibit991.htm)) yet is about 54% below its high.

CoreWeave and Nebius were down more than 30% in a month ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/markets/whats-fueling-wall-streets-growing-concerns-about-ai)).

The bigger picture on AI spending explains why fear has run ahead of the facts. Spending plans have not been cut. The big four cloud companies plan about **$720–750bn** of capital spending (capex, money spent on data centres and equipment) in 2026 ([Yield Theory](https://www.yieldtheory.app/research/hyperscaler-ai-capex-tracker-2026)). The worry has moved from demand to two other places:

- **Financing.** Big tech issued a record amount of bonds in 2026, and Goldman expects $420bn in 2027 ([Daily Caller](https://dailycaller.com/2026/09/22/hyperscaler-debt-financing-expected-to-reach-420-billion-next-year/)).
- **Politics.** Nothing on data-centre limits has actually been enacted yet.

Ed Yardeni's line, "better to let air come out of the AI bubble than let it burst", captures the more hopeful reading ([CNBC](https://cnbc.com/2026/11/04/democratic-data-center-pushback-could-help-ai-stocks.html)).

Central banks bought **321 tonnes** in the third quarter, up 42% on a year earlier, and holdings in gold ETFs reached a **record 4,256 tonnes** ([Kitco/WGC](http://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-11-03/gold-demand-remains-near-record-highs-central-bank-buying-surges-42-wgc); [SMM](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/104205484-world-gold-council-gold-demand-near-record-levels-central-banks-buy-321-tonnes-etfs-boom)). So the fall is about interest rates and investor positioning, not lost demand.

Gold's relationship with the war has flipped. It now trades on inflation and interest rates rather than as a safe haven. Counterintuitively, a peace deal that lowered oil and removed the pressure for rate hikes could *help* gold.

## Three scenarios hinge on oil, central banks and AI credit

The backdrop is still stretched. US shares sit on a Shiller **CAPE ratio**, which compares prices with ten years of inflation-adjusted profits, of about **41**. It has only been above 40 during the dot-com era ([usmacro](https://usmacro.com/indicator/shiller_cape)). Leverage is high:

- Margin debt (money borrowed to buy shares) hit a record $1.5 trillion in June ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/12/history-says-these-3-warning-signs-precede-major/)).
- Assets in leveraged ETFs reached a record of about $218bn.
- About half of retail options trading is now in contracts that expire the same day ([Citadel Securities](https://www.citadelsecurities.com/news-and-insights/global-market-intelligence/1h-2026-market-structure-flows/)).

That layer of borrowed money explains why single days such as 5 November and Friday's Korean circuit breaker can feed on themselves. The most likely route from a share-price bubble to a wider credit problem is a chain: AI cloud lenders such as CoreWeave, then the private-credit funds that finance them, then retail investors in those funds.

There is a contrarian case too. Readings of extreme fear have historically been decent buying signals over 6–12 months, though they can last for weeks in a falling market. CNN's gauge hit about 6 in March 2026 before markets recovered ([BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/investing/opinion/2026/11/05/why-investors-shouldnt-follow-the-crowd-stan-wong/)). Many AI suppliers are also still raising guidance.

## Keep the staged plan, and arm the 10% trigger

**Has the rule-of-thumb trigger fired?** Probably not, but it is closer. The earlier plan said to bring all remaining steps forward if the global market (VWRP or MSCI World) falls 10% from its high.

MSCI World closed at 4,862 on 30 October, 3.6% below its 52-week high of 5,044 ([MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/quote/index/MSCI-WORLD-107361487/)). Since then the S&P 500 has fallen about 3.0%, and the US is roughly 70% of the index. Europe and the UK are slightly up. Japan fell about 9% between 30 October and Friday's close. Weighting these moves gives an estimate of roughly **6–7% below the high**, or about 4,700–4,750. That is my arithmetic; no source reports it.

VWRP may be a little further below its high, perhaps 7–8%, for two reasons. It holds about 10% in emerging markets, including Korea and Taiwan. And it is priced in pounds, which have strengthened against the dollar. Friday's US session and the jobs report could move these figures either way.

**Check VWRP's current price against its 52-week closing high on your platform.** The trigger fires at −10%. That would take roughly one more US fall of 3–4%.

**What to do this week:**

- **If you have already made step 1** (half the money, into gilts, gold, money market funds and the core share funds), there is nothing to fix. Your global tracker is probably down a few per cent since purchase. Your UK, gilt and gold holdings are roughly flat or slightly up. The uninvested half is earning more now that Bank Rate is 4%.
- **If you have not**, nothing in this week's news argues against making step 1 now. The defensive half is unaffected by the tech crash, and the share half buys global equities at prices about 3% lower than a week ago.

**Should you speed up?** There is a real case for investing faster:

- Fear readings are extreme.
- On the earlier report's evidence, a lump sum beats phasing about two-thirds of the time ([Vanguard](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/cost_averaging_invest_now_or_temporarily_hold_your_cash.pdf)).
- Many of the market's best days come right after its worst.

There is an equally real case for staying staged:

- The Fed is still leaning towards raising rates rather than cutting them.
- Respected analysts see chips falling another 10–12%.
- The leverage stack can make falls feed on themselves.
- The two biggest near-term events, Nvidia on 17 November and UK inflation on 18 November, arrive in under two weeks.

The original schedule already places step 2 (20%) on about 19–20 November, right after both. The balanced answer is to **keep the schedule unchanged**: step 2 around 19–20 November, step 3 after the Fed on 9 December, step 4 in mid-January. Bring everything forward only if VWRP closes 10% or more below its high. A long-horizon investor comfortable with volatility could reasonably move step 2 up to now. Delaying beyond the schedule has no support in the evidence.

**What it means for the four example portfolios:**

- **Option A (Cautious).** Keeps its £2,100 money market reserve explicitly for the 10% trigger. It now earns more while it waits.
- **Option B (Balanced) and Option C (Growth, value-tilted).** The UK, value, equal-weight and gilt tilts are exactly what held up this week, so no change is needed.
- **Option B's emerging-markets fund (VFEG).** Took a hit because Taiwan and Korea make up about 39% of MSCI Emerging Markets ([MSCI](https://www.msci.com/downloads/web/msci-com/indexes/group/emerging-markets-indexes/MSCI%20Emerging%20Markets-cbr-en.pdf)). It also holds the China and India laggards, so it is now cheaper on both counts.
- **The gold sleeve.** The 5% gold holding in Options A, B and D now buys at about a 27% discount to the record rather than 12–23%. That is more attractive on value, but the same rate-hike pressure that caused the fall remains.

**Option D (Growth + AI robotics)** needs the most thought. Its RBTX robotics fund held Intel, AMD and Advantest as top holdings in May ([Trackinsight](https://www.trackinsight.com/en/fund/XLON%3ARBOT)), and all three were hit hard this fortnight. That confirms the earlier report's warning that robotics is a bet on the AI boom, not a hedge against it.

Anyone tempted by the memory crash should **swap within the existing 10% satellite rather than add to it**. For example, the £350 humanoid-robot holding (KOID) could become a single memory or storage share, or part of a semiconductor ETF, with no single holding above about £700 (5%). Spreading purchases over the remaining steps also means you do not have to guess the bottom.

**Use the unused £6,000 of 2026/27 allowance wisely.** It expires on 5 April 2027 and remains the natural reserve for a genuine correction, if you have spare cash.

## Conclusion

This sell-off is not punishing weak businesses. It is punishing the businesses with the strongest current results, because the market has stopped valuing them on this quarter's profits and started valuing them on 2027 risks: memory prices, politics and financing. For the AI supply chain, the most useful data over the next three months will therefore not be earnings, which will keep rising into the new year. It will be three other things: TrendForce's monthly memory price surveys, credit spreads on borrowers such as CoreWeave, and the capital-spending plans big tech announces in January. The memory makers are the clearest case. A forward P/E of 4 is either the bargain of the cycle or its warning sign, depending on what NAND and DRAM cost a year from now, and nobody yet knows.

For a UK investor, the week's real lesson is that the "boring" parts of the plan earned their place. The FTSE 100, European value shares and gilts were sneered at during the AI boom. They held near their highs while Seoul halted trading and Tokyo entered a bear market. With Bank Rate at 4%, waiting in a money market fund is paid, and keeping a 10% trigger in reserve costs very little. Patience is now cheap, so there is no need to guess the bottom or chase the bounce. Keep the schedule, check the trigger, and let Nvidia and UK inflation data, both due before step 2, do the deciding.
